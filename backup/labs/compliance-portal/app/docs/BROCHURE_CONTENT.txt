1. WHAT THE PRODUCT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Name:     DDS-CMS (Consent Management System)
Type:     Web-based privacy and DPDP compliance portal
Live at:  dds.sc.deeptrustxai.com

Purpose:
  Lets a business see its exact compliance position under India's Digital
  Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, across every platform it operates.

How it works:
  A company exposes one secure API endpoint containing its consent records.
  DDS-CMS reads that endpoint, identifies what personal data is present,
  applies the rules of that company's industry, and reports the compliance
  position — without storing or displaying the personal data itself.

Currently connected to two live platforms:
  - An e-commerce store (500 consent records)
  - A finance/banking platform (1,004 consent records)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. BACKGROUND: THE DPDP ACT, 2023
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Applies to any business processing personal data of people in India.
There is no exemption for small businesses.

Roles defined by the Act:
  Data Fiduciary  - the company deciding why and how personal data is
                    processed; carries the legal obligations
  Data Principal  - the individual the data belongs to
  Data Processor  - a third party processing data on the fiduciary's behalf;
                    the fiduciary remains accountable

Main obligations:
  Section 5      Give clear notice of purpose before processing
  Section 6      Collect only what the purpose requires, with valid consent
  Section 8      Maintain data accuracy; honour correction requests
  Section 8(5)   Apply reasonable security safeguards
  Section 8(6)   Report data breaches to the Board and affected individuals
  Section 9      Verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data;
                 no tracking or behavioural advertising directed at children
  Section 10     Extra duties for a Significant Data Fiduciary
  Section 12     Erase personal data once consent is withdrawn
  Section 13     Provide a grievance redressal mechanism

Maximum penalties (Schedule to the DPDP Act, 2023):
  Rs 250 crore   Failure to take reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5))
  Rs 200 crore   Failure to notify a data breach (s.8(6))
  Rs 200 crore   Non-compliance with children's data provisions (s.9)
  Rs 150 crore   Failure to meet Significant Data Fiduciary duties (s.10)
  Rs 10,000      Failure by a Data Principal to observe their duties (s.15)

  These are statutory maximums. The Data Protection Board decides the actual
  amount case by case. Penalties can apply separately per violation.

Notable change from the earlier regime:
  The Act removed the older "sensitive personal data" category. All personal
  data now receives the same level of protection — a customer's name is
  protected the same as their PAN.

Sectoral regulators add further requirements on top of DPDP:
  RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, TRAI and health-sector authorities each issue their own
  data-governance and data-localisation directives.

3. PROBLEMS THE PRODUCT ADDRESSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Compliance is usually claimed from policy documents, while the actual
  evidence sits in the data. Regulators examine the data.
- Consent state changes daily as customers register and withdraw, so a
  point-in-time audit is outdated almost immediately.
- Personal data is spread across multiple platforms, each with different
  fields and a different regulator.
- Manual checking is not feasible at volume: nobody can review a hundred
  thousand records for child records lacking guardian consent, or for
  withdrawals that were never actioned.
- Businesses often do not know which personal data they actually hold, as
  opposed to which they believe they hold.
- The obligations carrying the largest penalties are exactly those that can
  only be answered from the data.
- Third-party data sharing is frequently undocumented, despite s.8(2)
  requiring a contract with every processor.
- Many audit tools require direct database access, which itself creates a new
  data-exposure risk.

  Email address      format validation

  National Identifier, Financial Data, Health Data, Biometric, Credentials,

  Technical Identifier, Behavioural

  Driving licence, Demat/client code, Insurance policy, Medical record,

  Device identifier, Session identifier, Date of birth, Guardian details,
  Child status, Processing purpose, Order/usage history

  - Compliance flags and internal row identifiers are excluded, so PII counts
    reflect personal data only.
  - Each detected field carries a confidence level: "validated" where the value
    structure was confirmed, "name-only" where only the column name matched.
  - Credentials (passwords, tokens, PINs, OTPs) are flagged at maximum severity
    if found, as they should never appear in a consent record.

5. AUTOMATIC SECTOR DETECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The system identifies a company's industry sector by examining which
identifiers appear in its data, then applies that sector's regulator rules.

Examples:
  PAN, account numbers and balances present  ->  Banking & Payments (RBI)
  Address and order history present          ->  E-commerce & Retail
  ABHA ID and diagnoses present              ->  Healthcare

The detected sector and the reason for the detection are both reported.
A sector can also be set manually if preferred.

7. DPDP COMPLIANCE SCORING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ten statutory checks are assessed against the actual records:

   1. Consent status recorded for every principal
   2. Processing covered by an active consent
   3. Purpose specified (s.5 notice)
   4. PII erased when consent withdrawn (s.12)
   5. Erasure within the sector retention window
   6. Verifiable guardian consent for children (s.9)
   7. Collection limited to stated purpose (s.6)
   8. Data accuracy and correction rights (s.8)
   9. Reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5))
  10. Grievance redressal available (s.13)

Six of the ten can be measured from consent data alone. The remaining four
require evidence the consent API does not carry, and are reported as
"not measured" rather than counted as passed or failed.

Checks are weighted by consequence — erasure on withdrawal and children's
consent carry the heaviest weight.

The compliance score is a weighted average of what was actually measured, and
the system states how many of the ten checks that covers.

A separate privacy risk index (0-100) is calculated from the sensitivity of
detected fields, how much of the dataset they populate, and the compliance
findings. Each contributing factor and its point value are shown.

8. PRIVACY PROTECTION IN THE PRODUCT ITSELF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- The interface never displays personal data values. Names and email addresses
  are shown as "Present" or "Not provided".
- An auditor can confirm a record holds an email address without ever seeing
  the address.
- Masking previews use representative sample values, never real records.
- All access to client platforms is read-only (HTTP GET). The system cannot
  modify client data.
- No database credentials, VPN access or schema details are requested from a
  client.
- One API key maps to one company and one sector. An unrecognised key is
  rejected before any request is made.
- Revoking the key stops the integration immediately.
- Because no personal data is stored, there is no personal data to lose.

Technical note: the engine does read records in order to classify them (it
must, to validate a PAN or detect a child record), but it does not retain or
display the values.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. ERASURE AND ANONYMISATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- A record whose consent was withdrawn and whose personal data has been deleted
  is counted as a completed erasure.
- Time between withdrawal and erasure is measured against the sector's
  retention window; anything overdue is flagged.
- An integrity trail records that each erasure took place, without retaining
  the identifier it applied to.
- Success rate, pending erasures and most recent erasure are all reported.

Recommended masking technique by data class:
  Tokenise      national identifiers, financial data, health data, credentials
  Hash          contact details used only for matching
  Mask          identity information and children's data
  Generalise    location and behavioural data

Critical and high-risk fields are pre-selected for masking; lower-risk fields
are not, so the default action is proportionate.

10. THIRD-PARTY PROCESSOR TRACKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section 8(2) of the DPDP Act requires a valid contract with every processor
handling personal data on the fiduciary's behalf.

Processors can be declared by the company through its own API, or registered
by an operator in the portal. Each is assessed, not merely listed:

  No data processing agreement          ->  Violation (s.8(2))
  Expired agreement                     ->  Violation, regardless of
                                            destination
  Cross-border transfer with a
  contract in place                     ->  Flagged for review, destination
                                            named
  Purpose or data categories
  not declared                          ->  Flagged under s.5

Anything outside India counts as a cross-border transfer.

If no processors are declared, this is reported as "not declared" rather than
as a clean result, since the two are different claims.

11. HOW A COMPANY CONNECTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four steps, typically half a day of engineering work:

  1. The company generates an API key.
  2. It exposes one authenticated GET endpoint returning its consent records.
  3. It runs three provided checks: valid key returns data; missing key and
     wrong key are both rejected.
  4. It sends the endpoint URL and the API key. Nothing else.

Required fields per record (4):
  user_id, email, consent_status, timestamp

Fields that enable the compliance checks (6):
  purpose, consent_granted_at, consent_revoked_at, pii_deleted_at,
  is_minor, guardian_verified

Any additional personal-data fields are recognised automatically by name.

Connection parameters:
  Request method     GET (read-only)
  Authentication     x-api-key header, or Authorization: Bearer
  Response timeout   20 seconds
  Refresh interval   every 5 minutes
  Records per fetch  500 (configurable)

An onboarding pack (documentation plus a generated key) can be produced per
client with a single command.

12. MODULES IN THE PORTAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Eight modules:

  Command Centre        Compliance index, consent breakdown, PII risk
                        distribution, erasure activity, action items
  Platform Integration  Connect and manage platforms, sector selection,
                        synchronisation log
  Consent Management    Consent record registry, requests, revocations,
                        analytics
  DPDP Compliance       The ten statutory checks with per-control pass rates
  PII Intelligence      Field inventory, risk index, industry benchmarking,
                        data lifecycle view
  Anonymisation         Masking workspace, erasure jobs, third-party sharing
                        register, integrity trail
  Reports               Exportable compliance evidence
  Settings              Platform configuration and sector packs

Additional capability:
  A cross-sector view shows every connected platform on one screen, with
  record-weighted compliance and risk across the whole group.

14. TECHNICAL AND DEPLOYMENT FACTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Frontend              React
  Backend               Node.js / Express
  Deployment            Single Docker container, hosted on Dokploy
  Database              Not required (optional, for audit history only)
  API endpoints         41
  Portal modules        8
  Sector packs          7
  Identifier detectors  32
  Compliance checks     10

Operational characteristics:
  - Stateless: configuration comes from environment variables and every score
    is computed at runtime, so a redeploy always returns to a current state.
  - Runs unprivileged with a built-in health check.
  - Adding a company requires one environment entry: key, endpoint, sector,
    label.
  - One failing platform never affects the others; each is analysed and
    reported independently.
  - A platform whose key is rejected is reported as needing attention, with the
    host and the reason named.
